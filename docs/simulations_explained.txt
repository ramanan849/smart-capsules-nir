BB601 Team 10 - what each simulation is, why we run it, and what came out

All code is in sims/. To rebuild every figure and number from scratch:
    python sims/run_all.py
Figures land in sims/figs/ (PNG for docs/slides, PDF for print), numbers in sims/results/.
Every input and where it comes from: sims/results/parameter_table.txt ("NO" = not checked
against a primary source).

All nine validation checks pass (sims/results/validation.json).

1. THE KILL-SWITCH CHECK            fig_killswitch.png        (heat_killswitch.py)
------------------------------------------------------------
What it is: The simplest possible heat calculation. A warm sphere in water or tissue settles
at ΔT = (absorbed power) / (4π k R). We do it twice - once for one carrier on its own, and once
for a whole tumour full of carriers (a 1 cm ball of tissue, heated evenly, cooled by blood).

Why we run it first: Every triggered-release paper we read is micron-scale, where one capsule
can heat itself. Our carrier is 100 times smaller. If it cannot reach 41-43 °C at a laser power
that is safe for skin, the whole nanoscale design fails, and we would want to know that now
rather than in week three.

What we wanted: A yes or no, and if yes, how much gold it takes.

What came out:
- One carrier alone (30 rods, at the 0.33 W/cm² skin limit): about 0.0001 K. A clear no.
  Even a 5 µm Skirtach capsule only gets about 0.004 K at this intensity - Skirtach got his
  5-15 K with a focused 50 mW beam under a microscope, which is far above any skin limit.
- Many carriers together: yes. About 7 µg of rod gold per gram of tumour gives +5 K at the
  centre (6-9 µg/g over the range of blood flow). Nunes measured 41 µg/g in tumours after an
  IV dose, so this is within reach, and easier for us since we deliver locally.
- With spheres instead of rods it takes about 580 µg/g. Not realistic.

So what: At our size the heating is collective. The gold on the carrier matters because it goes
wherever the drug goes, not because each carrier makes a hot spot around itself. This also
means the "steep local gradient at the shell" idea from our Approach does not work at 140 nm.

Caveats: Light is assumed to reach the whole tumour at full strength (see sensitivity). Rods
are treated as independent absorbers. Tissue's own absorption is ignored.

2. OPTICS: SPHERES OR RODS?         fig_resonance_vs_loading.png, fig_optics_spectra.png   (optics.py)
------------------------------------------------------------
What it is: How strongly gold absorbs light, and at what colour, for (a) one sphere (Mie theory,
from the miepython library, plus the simple quasi-static formula as a check), (b) one rod (Gans
theory - a rod treated as a stretched sphere), and (c) many spheres sitting on our carrier and
talking to each other (coupled dipoles: each sphere feels the light plus the fields of all the
others). Gold data: Johnson and Christy 1972, with the extra damping that small particles have.

Why: Our original rationale assumed that packing spheres densely red-shifts them into the NIR.
We said in Submission 2 we would let the optics decide between spheres and rods.

What we wanted: The absorption peak against how much gold is on the carrier (filling factor Fs)
for spheres, and against aspect ratio for rods, compared with our 800-830 nm window.

What came out:
- Checks: a bare 5 nm sphere in water peaks at 523 nm (Govorov says ~520). Mie and quasi-static
  agree within 1% at that size.
- Spheres on a 130 nm carrier: the peak only moves from ~530 to ~550-555 nm, even at Fs = 0.4,
  even when we let them clump. Never near 800 nm.
- Rods: aspect ratio 3.8-4.1 puts the peak in 800-830 nm (for a surrounding index of 1.45;
  4.3-4.6 in plain water, 3.6-3.9 at 1.5). A 40 x 10 nm rod peaks at 820 nm.
- Per unit of gold, rods absorb about 68 times more than spheres at 808 nm.
Answer: rods.

Caveats: The dipole model underestimates coupling when spheres actually touch, so tightly
fused clusters (like Angelatos after irradiation) could shift further - but that is not a
state we can design for. Gans theory is quasi-static; real rods shift a bit further red.

3. HEAT MODEL CHECKS                fig_heat_checks.png        (heat_checks.py)
------------------------------------------------------------
What it is: Two checks of our heat model against the only numbers the literature gives us.
Left: one gold sphere at 10^4 W/cm², surface rise against radius. Right: Skirtach's capsules -
how the heating slope changes when the gold filling factor goes from 0.2 to 0.4.

Why: Before we trust the kill-switch number we want the model to reproduce known results.

What came out:
- ΔT ∝ R²: slope 2.00, as Govorov says.
- Govorov Fig. 1b (R = 30 nm): we get 5.1 K, he shows about 4.3 K. 18% apart, which is fine
  for reading a number off a plot.
- Skirtach: his own nearest-neighbour model predicts 1.41x, adding up every particle on the
  capsule predicts 2.0x, and he measured 1.58x. From his error bars the measured ratio is about
  1.58 ± 0.27, so the data cannot really tell the two models apart. Not to forget, at the scale
  of a whole capsule the "add everything up" picture is the physically complete one, and
  clustering of the gold (visible in his TEM) would pull it below 2.

4. HOW FAST THINGS HEAT UP          fig_tau_vs_size.png        (heat_checks.py)
------------------------------------------------------------
What it is: The time-dependent heat equation for a sphere that starts absorbing at t = 0,
solved numerically.

Why: In Submission 2 we used τ ≈ L²/κ on the back of an envelope to throw out the pulsed regime.
This checks it properly.

What came out: Time to reach 63% of steady state is about 2.1 R²/κ. That is 0.13 ns for a 3 nm
gold particle, 73 ns for our carrier, and about 0.1 ms for a 5 µm capsule. During a 10 ns pulse
our carrier only gets to about a quarter of its steady rise, and the 5 µm capsule gets basically
nothing. That confirms why we went with continuous-wave light.

5. TEMPERATURE IN TISSUE (PENNES)   fig_pennes.png             (pennes.py)
------------------------------------------------------------
What it is: The Pennes bioheat equation, which is the heat equation plus a cooling term for blood
flow. A 1 cm tumour full of carriers inside normal tissue, laser on for 15 min, then off.

Why: To answer the question from our very first rationale that we never answered - how far does
the heat spread into healthy tissue? Also to get the dwell time.

What came out (8 µg/g gold, 0.33 W/cm²):
- Centre: 41 °C in about 1.6 min, settles at about 42.4 °C.
- Tumour edge: about 40 °C. 2 mm into normal tissue: about 38.7 °C.
- Same gold at the O'Neal condition (4 W/cm², 3 min): the centre goes past 90 °C. So the
  literature condition is both over the skin limit and far past mild hyperthermia for us.
- Check: the numerical centre temperature matches the exact formula to 0.02%.

Caveat: The edge of the tumour runs about 2 °C cooler than the centre. That is just how a heated
ball in tissue behaves, and it means "41-43 °C" really applies to the core of the tumour.

6. DRUG RELEASE                     fig_release.png            (release.py)
------------------------------------------------------------
What it is: The fraction of drug out against time. The liposome lets drug out slowly at 37 °C
and very fast above its transition (~40-41 °C). The polyelectrolyte wall is a second barrier in
series with an unknown permeability. We drive it with the temperature from the Pennes run.

Why: The release has to reproduce a no-laser baseline before any triggered prediction means
anything.

What came out:
- Baseline: matched to Deng's doxorubicin from the coated liposome, 11% at 24 h (Fig. 6B).
  After 24 h Deng's curve speeds up (their Korsmeyer-Peppas n is about 1.1, i.e. not simple
  diffusion), which our simple model does not follow. Deng's siRNA curve (Fig. 2E) is shown
  for comparison only - it is siRNA coming off the film, not drug.
- With the laser: 90% released at the tumour centre within about 2 min, so the dwell time is
  set by the heating, not the lipid.
- The wall only matters if its doxorubicin permeability is below about 4 x 10⁻⁸ cm/s (see
  fig_sensitivity.png, right). Nobody has measured it.

Caveat: Deng's liposome is rigid DSPC/cholesterol. A thermosensitive liposome will leak more at
37 °C, so the baseline is a best case.

7. SAFETY                           fig_safety.png             (safety.py)
------------------------------------------------------------
What it is: (a) The continuous-wave skin limit against wavelength, with our protocol and the
literature conditions marked. (b) The thermal dose CEM43 (minutes-equivalent at 43 °C) along the
Pennes temperature history.

What came out:
- Skin limit (exposures > 10 s): 0.33 W/cm² at 808 nm, 0.36 at 830 nm. Ours sits on it.
  O'Neal's 4 W/cm² is about 12x over. Angelatos' average 7 W/cm² at 1064 nm is about 7x over.
- CEM43 after 15 min: ~4.5 min at the tumour centre (mild hyperthermia), ~0.2 at the edge,
  ~0.03 at 2 mm into normal tissue.
- Size and Reissner: 100 nm core + 4 layers = 148 nm total, wall h/R about 0.18 (0.3 with rods),
  which is still outside Fery's h/R < 0.1, so we do not quote a stiffness.

8. SENSITIVITY AND THE DESIGN ENVELOPE   fig_sensitivity.png, results/design_envelope.txt   (design.py)
------------------------------------------------------------
What it is: Change one input at a time and see how the tumour-centre temperature moves (left), and
how release time depends on the unknown wall permeability (right). Then a grid search for the
ranges that give 41-43 °C at the centre under the skin limit.

What came out:
- Biggest effects: total gold in the tumour (4-16 µg/g -> 39.7-47.8 °C) and tumour size
  (6-15 mm -> 39.3-47.0 °C). Then how much light reaches the tumour (half the light -> 39.7 °C).
  Blood flow and tissue conductivity move it by about 1 °C each way.
- The rod aspect ratio has to be matched to the actual index around the rods - an aspect-4 rod
  in n = 1.33 or 1.5 instead of 1.45 loses about 1 °C.
- Envelope (also in Section III.c of the doc):
    gold        : nanorods ~40 x 10 nm, aspect ratio 3.8-4.1, 20-50 per carrier
    carrier     : 100 nm core + 4 PSS/PAH layers + rods + HA, ~148 nm
    irradiance  : <= 0.33 W/cm² at 808 nm
    tumour gold : 6-9 µg/g at 0.33 W/cm² (5.5-10.5 over the blood flow range);
                  10-15 at 0.2 W/cm²; 20-29 at 0.1 W/cm²
    dwell       : ~1.5-3 min to reach 41 °C, release done within about 2 min of switching on;
                  10-15 min total for the hyperthermia dose
